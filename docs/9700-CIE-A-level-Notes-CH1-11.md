## Chapter 1: Cell Structure

### Microscopy
1. **Light microscope: can X be seen?**
   - Low resolution
   - Can only distinguish structures larger than about 200 nm
   - If X is smaller than this, it cannot be seen clearly

2. **TEM and SEM**
   - Higher resolution than light microscope
   - TEM: 2D internal detail
   - SEM: 3D surface detail

3. **Advantages of TEM/SEM over light microscope**
   - Higher magnification
   - Higher resolution
   - Can see organelles in more detail

4. **Estimating magnification**
   - Measure image length on figure
   - Convert to same unit, usually mm
   - $$1\,\mu m = 10^{-3}\,mm$$
   - Magnification = image size / actual size

5. **Why some cells appear darker/lighter**
   - Different parts stain differently
   - Different densities due to amount of organelles

### Viruses
- Protein coat
- DNA or RNA
- Acellular

### Cell Structures and Organelles
1. **Extra details of nucleus seen with electron microscope**
   - Heterochromatin and euchromatin
   - Nuclear pores
   - Nuclear envelope / double membrane

2. **Role of acid hydrolases in lysosomes**
   - Hydrolyse proteins
   - Destroy organelles
   - Destroy pathogens
   - Recycle materials

3. **Structure and function of RER / SER**
   - RER: ribosomes, flattened sacs, protein synthesis and transport
   - SER: no ribosomes, tubular shape, lipid/cholesterol/steroid synthesis

4. **Advantages of enzymes attached to SER membrane**
   - More efficient synthesis of lipids/cholesterol/steroids
   - Membranes provide protection and support

5. **Golgi body structure**
   - Flattened sacs / cisternae
   - Stacked / layered structure

6. **Why chloroplasts are near cell edge**
   - Large central vacuole pushes cytoplasm outward
   - Chloroplasts remain around periphery

7. **How nucleus is suited to containing DNA**
   - Nuclear envelope separates DNA from cytoplasm
   - Nuclear pores regulate exchange
   - Protects DNA from hydrolytic enzymes

8. **How proteins are broken down in vacuoles / lysosomes**
   - Protease enzymes
   - Hydrolysis
   - Peptide bonds broken
   - Products are peptides / amino acids

9. **Role of lysosomes in defense**
   - Pathogens enclosed in vesicles / phagosomes
   - Fuse with lysosomes to form phagolysosomes
   - Hydrolytic enzymes digest pathogens

10. **Flagellum structure**
   - Microtubules present
   - $$9 + 2$$ arrangement

11. **Distinguishing eukaryotes and prokaryotes**
   - Eukaryotes have membrane-bound organelles
   - Eukaryotes have nucleus / nuclear envelope
   - Prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles
   - Size difference

12. **Euchromatin vs heterochromatin**
   - Euchromatin: lighter, genes more active, transcription occurs
   - Heterochromatin: darker, tightly packed, less transcriptionally active

13. **Advantages of breakdown in lysosomes / peroxisomes**
   - Membrane bounds harmful enzymes
   - Protects rest of cell
   - High local concentration of enzymes
   - Suitable internal conditions maintained

14. **Features of prokaryotes**
   - Peptidoglycan cell wall
   - Circular DNA
   - No membrane-bound organelles

### Cell Transport
1. **How X enters cell**
   - Binds to receptor on cell membrane
   - Complementary shape / binding site
   - Endocytosis
   - Membrane infolds and forms vesicle

2. **How vesicles leave cell**
   - Vesicle moves to cell surface membrane
   - Via cytoskeleton / microtubules
   - Vesicle fuses with membrane
   - Exocytosis

3. **Why different concentrations change mass of tissue**
   - Different concentrations create water potential differences
   - Higher solute concentration means lower water potential
   - Water moves from higher to lower water potential

4. **Roles of carrier proteins**
   - Transport polar / water-soluble substances
   - Used in active transport and facilitated diffusion
   - Have specific binding sites
   - Undergo conformational change

5. **Roles of channel proteins**
   - Transport ions / some polar molecules
   - Facilitate diffusion down gradient
   - Hydrophilic channel
   - Some are gated
   - Aquaporins are water channels

6. **How carrier proteins maintain low pH in lysosome**
   - Move ions against concentration gradient
   - Active transport using ATP

7. **Features of active transport**
   - Against concentration gradient
   - Uses ATP
   - Requires carrier proteins
   - Specific binding sites
   - Conformational change

8. **Cotransport**
   - One substance moves down gradient
   - Powers movement of another against gradient

9. **Advantages of large gaps between endothelial cells**
   - Shorter diffusion distance
   - Faster movement of molecules
   - Larger molecules can pass through

## Chapter 2: Biological Molecules

1. **Amylose vs amylopectin**
   - Similarities:
     - Both polysaccharides
     - Both made of alpha-glucose
     - Both have $$1,4$$ glycosidic bonds
   - Differences:
     - Amylose unbranched and helical
     - Amylopectin branched
     - Amylopectin has $$1,6$$ glycosidic bonds
     - Amylopectin more compact

2. **Cellulose molecule and microfibril**
   - Cellulose molecule:
     - Beta-glucose
     - Alternate monomers rotated $$180^\circ$$
     - Straight unbranched chain
     - Glycosidic bonds
   - Microfibril:
     - Parallel cellulose chains
     - Hydrogen bonds between chains

3. **Why some proteins have all four levels of structure**
   - More than one polypeptide chain
   - Each chain has primary, secondary and tertiary structure
   - Multiple chains give quaternary structure

4. **How collagen polypeptide is suited to collagen molecule formation**
   - Fibrous protein
   - Glycine is small
   - Triple helix tightly packed
   - Hydrogen bonds form between adjacent chains
   - Gives strength

5. **How triglyceride structure suits energy storage**
   - Three fatty acids with long hydrocarbon chains
   - High ratio of C-H bonds
   - Compact / dense energy store
   - Large energy per unit mass

6. **Advantages of using a colorimeter**
   - Improves accuracy
   - Gives quantitative results

7. **Meaning of gene mutation**
   - Change in sequence of base pairs in DNA
   - May code for altered polypeptide

8. **Disaccharide**
   - Two monosaccharides joined
   - Glycosidic bond
   - Formed by condensation
   - Water released

9. **How glycogen structure suits function**
   - Highly branched
   - Many free ends for rapid hydrolysis
   - Compact for storage
   - Insoluble so does not affect water potential

10. **Lipids and melting point**
   - Longer chain and more saturated = higher melting point
   - Shorter chain and more unsaturated = lower melting point

11. **How collagen structure suits mammalian function**
   - High tensile strength
   - Triple helix with many hydrogen bonds
   - Polypeptides parallel
   - Covalent cross-links between molecules
   - Staggered ends increase strength

## Chapter 3: Enzymes

1. **Effect of mutation when $$V_{max}$$ lower but $$K_m$$ unchanged**
   - Amino acid sequence changed
   - Tertiary structure of active site altered
   - Substrate can still bind
   - Catalysis less effective

2. **Describe data with reference to figure**
   - Give overall trend
   - Mention change in gradient
   - Support with data

3. **Explain enzyme graph**
   - At low substrate concentration, many active sites available
   - More substrate gives more collisions
   - More enzyme-substrate complexes form
   - Rate increases
   - At high substrate concentration, active sites occupied
   - Number of active sites does not increase

4. **Relationship between enzyme and two substrates**
   - Enzyme has higher affinity for one substrate if it binds more readily

5. **Why immobilized enzyme may show smaller activity at $$25^\circ C$$**
   - Immobilization may alter tertiary structure
   - Lower collision frequency with substrate

1. **How addition of saturated fatty acid chain allows molecule to act as signalling molecule**
   - Forms hydrophobic region
   - Helps bind to receptor
   - Complementary shape to receptor
   - Affects reactions inside cell

2. **Cell signalling inside cell**
   - Ligand secreted
   - Binds receptor
   - Activates G protein
   - G protein activates second messenger
   - Second messenger activates enzymes
   - Cascade amplifies response

3. **How pathogens are destroyed by lysosomes**
   - Taken in by phagocytosis
   - Form phagocytic vesicle
   - Lysosome fuses with vesicle
   - Enzymes digest pathogen
   - Useful products absorbed / components recycled

4. **How endocytosis occurs**
   - Substance attaches to receptors
   - Membrane infolds
   - Membrane pinches off to form vesicle

## Chapter 5: Cell Division, Cancer, Stem Cells

1. **Cancer**
   - Carcinogen may cause mutation
   - Cells divide without control

2. **Importance of mitosis**
   - Replaces old cells
   - Repairs tissue
   - Produces genetically identical cells
   - Growth

3. **Interphase summary**
   - G1: protein synthesis
   - S: DNA replication
   - G2: microtubule synthesis, organelle replication, checking for errors

4. **Role of telomeres**
   - Prevent loss of genetic information

5. **Role of stem cells**
   - Unspecialised
   - Divide by mitosis
   - Differentiate into specialised cell types
   - Repair / replace cells

6. **Role of centromere**
   - Holds sister chromatids together
   - Attachment point for spindle fibres
   - Helps move chromatids to poles

7. **Structure of chromosomes**
   - Two identical sister chromatids
   - Joined by centromere
   - Each chromatid contains one DNA molecule
   - Telomeres at ends
   - DNA associated with histone proteins

## Chapter 6: Nucleic Acids and Protein Synthesis

1. **Outline protein synthesis**
   - Transcription
   - Translation:
     - tRNA carries specific amino acid
     - Anticodon binds codon on mRNA
     - Complementary base pairing
     - Peptide bonds form
     - Ribosome moves along mRNA

2. **Why only one DNA strand is used**
   - Only one strand is template strand
   - mRNA is single-stranded
   - mRNA sequence used to make polypeptide

3. **Meaning of gene mutation**
   - Change in DNA base sequence
   - May cause different polypeptide

4. **Why different primary structures may have similar function**
   - Similar tertiary structure
   - Similar binding site shape

5. **DNA replication, elongation details**
   - Complementary base pairing
   - Hydrogen bonds between bases
   - Phosphodiester bond forms between sugar and phosphate
   - Nucleotides added to $$3'$$ end

6. **Antiparallel and new strand synthesis**
   - Strands parallel but opposite directions
   - New strands made in $$5' \to 3'$$ direction
   - Leading strand continuous
   - Lagging strand formed as Okazaki fragments
   - DNA ligase joins fragments

7. **Importance of RNA splicing**
   - Removes introns
   - Joins exons
   - Produces mature mRNA

8. **Differences between introns and exons**
   - Introns removed from pre-mRNA
   - Exons remain in mature mRNA

9. **Why DNA replication is semi-conservative**
   - Each new DNA molecule has one original strand and one new strand

## Chapter 7: Transport in Plants

1. **How transfer of sucrose into phloem leads to transport**
   - Sucrose enters sieve tube
   - Water potential becomes more negative
   - Water enters by osmosis
   - Volume increases
   - Hydrostatic pressure increases
   - Sap moves from high to low hydrostatic pressure

2. **Why unloading helps mass flow**
   - Sucrose removed at sink
   - Water potential rises
   - Water leaves sieve tube
   - Hydrostatic pressure at sink decreases
   - Pressure gradient maintained

3. **How water moves up xylem in continuous column**
   - Cohesion between water molecules by hydrogen bonds
   - Adhesion to xylem walls
   - Transpiration pull
   - Water column maintained

4. **How parenchyma / turgor supports plant**
   - Water enters by osmosis
   - Large vacuole fills
   - Cell becomes turgid
   - Outward pressure gives support

5. **How root hairs adapt for water absorption**
   - Thin
   - Long
   - Large surface area to volume ratio

6. **Why a root can be both source and sink**
   - Source when supplying assimilates
   - Sink when storing assimilates
   - Phloem transport can be bidirectional

7. **Mechanism for phloem sap movement**
   - Assimilates loaded into sieve tube elements
   - Water enters by osmosis
   - Pressure builds up
   - Mass flow occurs
   - At sink, sucrose removed and water also removed

8. **Transpiration pull**
   - Loss of water vapour from leaves

9. **Sequence of assimilate loading from apoplast**
   - Protons pumped out by active transport
   - Proton gradient forms in apoplast
   - Protons move back via cotransporter
   - Sucrose / amino acids enter companion cell
   - Then move into sieve tube element

## Chapter 8: Transport in Mammals

1. **Sequence of events in heart**
   - SAN releases impulse
   - Impulses spread across atria
   - Atria contract
   - AV valve opens / blood enters ventricles
   - AVN delays then sends impulse down Purkyne tissue
   - Ventricles contract
   - Semilunar valves open

2. **Ventricular diastole**
   - Ventricular pressure falls
   - Semilunar valves close
   - Atria fill with blood
   - AV valves open
   - Blood enters ventricles

3. **Flow of blood through left side of heart**
   - Oxygenated blood enters left atrium via pulmonary vein
   - AV valve opens
   - Blood enters left ventricle
   - Ventricle contracts
   - Semilunar valve opens
   - Blood enters aorta

4. **Why left side is thicker**
   - More cardiac muscle
   - Pumps blood further distance
   - Generates greater pressure

5. **Three ways arteries differ from veins**
   - Thicker muscle layer
   - Smaller lumen
   - More regular shape

6. **How $$CO_2$$ and $$H^+$$ help unloading of oxygen**
   - Haemoglobin binds $$H^+$$
   - Tertiary structure changes
   - Reduced affinity for oxygen
   - Oxygen released more easily
   - Bohr effect

7. **How haemoglobin transports $$CO_2$$**
   - $$CO_2$$ combines with amino groups
   - Forms carbaminohaemoglobin
   - Released in alveoli where $$CO_2$$ is low

8. **Bohr effect diagram**
   - Curve shifts right
   - Use data if figure given

9. **Formation of tissue fluid**
   - High hydrostatic pressure at arterial end forces fluid out
   - Large proteins and RBC remain in blood
   - At venous end, lower hydrostatic pressure allows water to return

## Chapter 9: Gas Exchange

1. **Function of elastic fibres in lung tissue**
   - Allow alveoli to stretch and recoil
   - Help air move out
   - Prevent bursting

2. **Function of smooth muscle**
   - Contracts and relaxes
   - Changes airway diameter
   - Regulates airflow

3. **Distribution of cartilage**
   - C-shaped rings in trachea
   - Irregular blocks / plates in bronchi

4. **Functions of cartilage**
   - Keeps airways open
   - Support
   - Flexibility

5. **Structural features of tracheal epithelium**
   - Ciliated epithelial cells
   - Columnar cells
   - Goblet cells

6. **Difference between blood to lungs and blood from aorta**
   - Pulmonary blood lower in oxygen, higher in $$CO_2$$
   - Aortic blood higher in oxygen, lower in $$CO_2$$

7. **How trachea / bronchus / bronchioles protect against pathogens**
   - Mucus traps pathogens
   - Cilia move mucus away from lungs
   - Phagocytes can destroy trapped pathogens

8. **Capillary features**
   - One cell thick wall
   - Narrow diameter
   - RBC pass single file
   - No muscle or elastic tissue

9. **Function of capillary to smooth muscle**
   - Deliver oxygen / nutrients
   - Remove waste / $$CO_2$$
   - Thin wall shortens diffusion distance

10. **Differences between trachea, bronchus, bronchiole, alveolus**
   - Trachea has cartilage rings
   - Bronchi have cartilage plates
   - Bronchioles have no cartilage
   - Alveoli are thin-walled and adapted for gas exchange

11. **How alveoli are adapted for gas exchange**
   - Very thin walls
   - Large surface area
   - Rich capillary supply
   - Elastic fibres / connective tissue

12. **Why trachea, bronchi and some bronchioles do not carry out gas exchange**
   - Diffusion distance too great
   - Airflow too rapid
   - Blood supply not specialised for gas exchange

## Chapter 10: Infectious Diseases

1. **Examples**
   - Cholera: bacterium, *Vibrio cholerae*, transmitted by contaminated food/water
   - Malaria: protoctist, *Plasmodium malariae*, transmitted by female *Anopheles* mosquito
   - Tuberculosis: bacterium, *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* / *M. bovis*, droplet infection
   - HIV/AIDS: virus, human immunodeficiency virus, sexual contact

2. **Why vaccination limits spread**
   - Herd immunity
   - Large proportion immune
   - Fewer susceptible people encounter infected person

3. **How to promote vaccination programme**
   - Make vaccine available and stable
   - Vaccinate early
   - Educate public
   - Disease should mutate less if programme is to remain effective

4. **Factors to prevent sexual transmission of HIV**
   - Condom availability and use
   - Sex education
   - Resource availability
   - Circumcision prevalence may influence spread

5. **Why something is an infectious disease**
   - Caused by pathogen
   - Transmissible
   - Can pass from infected to uninfected person

6. **Role of antibiotics**
   - Kill bacteria / inhibit bacterial growth
   - Do not affect viruses directly
   - Can reduce transmission if treatment successful

7. **How malaria is passed on**
   - Female mosquito feeds on infected person
   - Then bites uninfected person
   - Pathogen transmitted in saliva

8. **Effect of penicillin on bacterium**
   - Inhibits transpeptidase
   - Prevents cross-linking of peptidoglycan
   - Cell wall weakened
   - Cell may burst

9. **Figure questions**
   - Give overall trend
   - Support with data
   - Identify maxima / minima if relevant

10. **How antibodies reduce spread in bloodstream**
   - Bind specifically to antigens
   - Complementary shape
   - Prevent pathogen entering RBC / cells
   - Help phagocytes destroy pathogen

## Chapter 11: Immunity

1. **Why plasma cells contain lots of rough ER**
   - Plasma cells secrete antibodies
   - Antibodies are proteins
   - Rough ER has ribosomes for protein synthesis

2. **Why vaccine can provide long-term protection**
   - Memory cells produced
   - Memory cells are long-lived
   - Faster secondary immune response
   - More antibodies produced on second exposure

3. **Why passive treatment does not prevent future infection**
   - Passive immunity
   - Antibodies short-lived
   - No memory cells formed
   - Only short-term protection

4. **Definition of non-self antigen**
   - Foreign molecule / usually protein
   - Triggers immune response

5. **Features of non-self antigens**
   - Stimulate immune response
   - Often proteins
   - Recognised by immune cells

6. **Meaning of transmissible**
   - Pathogen can pass from one person to another

7. **How B lymphocytes recognise non-self antigens**
   - Antigen binds specific receptor on B cell
   - Complementary shape

8. **Immune system fails to distinguish self and non-self**
   - Autoimmune disease
   - Self tissues attacked

9. **Reasons vaccination programme was successful**
   - Pathogen did not mutate much
   - High vaccine coverage
   - Cheap / easy production and distribution

10. **Types of immunity**
   - Active / passive
   - Natural / artificial

11. **Roles of macrophages / monocytes**
   - Phagocytosis
   - Recognise antigens
   - Present antigens

12. **Roles of B cells**
   - Recognise antigen
   - Divide by mitosis
   - Differentiate into plasma cells
   - Produce antibodies

13. **Roles of killer T cells**
   - Kill infected cells
   - Can use perforin / enzymes
   - May trigger apoptosis

14. **Role of T helper lymphocytes**
   - Secrete cytokines
   - Activate B cells
   - Stimulate phagocytes

15. **Hybridoma method for monoclonal antibodies**
   - Inject antigen into small mammal
   - Immune response occurs
   - Remove plasma cells from spleen
   - Fuse with myeloma cells
   - Hybridoma clones produce antibodies

16. **Role of phagocytes**
   - Ingest bacteria
   - May help activate lymphocytes

17. **Role of memory T lymphocytes**
   - Long-term immunity
   - Faster secondary response

18. **Danger of widespread antibiotic use**
   - Selects for resistant bacteria
   - Antibiotics may become ineffective

19. **Role of antigen-presenting cells**
   - Present antigens to lymphocytes
   - Help activate correct immune cells

20. **Difference between primary and secondary response**
   - Secondary faster
   - More memory cells involved
   - Higher antibody concentration
   - Pathogen eliminated faster

21. **Why phagocytes act against bacteria not human cells**
   - Human cells have self antigens
   - Bacteria have non-self antigens
   - Non-self triggers immune response

22. **Why some pathogens survive in phagocytes**
   - Produce enzyme inhibitors
   - Resist hydrolytic enzymes

23. **Why people with HIV/AIDS are more susceptible to infection**
   - HIV attacks T helper cells / CD4 cells
   - Number of lymphocytes reduced
   - Immune response weakened

24. **Why something is a disorder of immune system**
   - Autoimmune response
   - Failure to distinguish self and non-self

25. **Why vaccines may not reach every individual**
   - Access / logistics
   - Cost / storage
   - Medical contraindications
   - Hesitancy

26. **Epitope and antigen binding site**
   - Epitope is on antigen
   - Antigen-binding site is on antibody

27. **Why antibodies differ**
   - Different tertiary structures
   - Different binding site shapes
   - Specific complementary binding

28. **How antigen-presenting cell activates immune response**
   - Antigen displayed on cell surface
   - Complementary receptors on B / T cells bind
   - Activates lymphocytes

29. **Advantages of vaccines**
   - Artificial active immunity
   - Strong primary response
   - Memory cells formed
   - Long-lasting protection
   - Herd immunity
