## Chapter 12: Respiration

1. **How mitochondrial structure is related to function**
   - Function: ATP production
   - Double membrane
   - Inner membrane folded into cristae to increase surface area
   - Site of oxidative phosphorylation
   - ATP synthase present
   - Electron transport chain / carrier proteins present
   - Intermembrane space allows proton accumulation, low pH
   - Proton gradient between intermembrane space and matrix
   - Matrix contains enzymes for link reaction and Krebs cycle
   - Mitochondrial DNA and ribosomes for protein synthesis
   - Outer membrane permeable to pyruvate, NADH, oxygen

2. **Sites of stages of respiration**
   - Glycolysis: cytoplasm
   - Link reaction: matrix
   - Krebs cycle: matrix
   - Oxidative phosphorylation: inner mitochondrial membrane

3. **Need for energy**
   - Active transport
   - Vesicle movement / protein transport
   - Anabolic reactions such as DNA / protein synthesis

4. **Why ATP is suitable as universal energy currency**
   - Hydrolysed quickly and easily
   - Releases a useful quantity of energy
   - Stable at normal pH
   - Broken down by ATPase
   - Can be regenerated
   - Small and can diffuse within cells

5. **ATP synthesis**
   - Substrate-level phosphorylation: phosphate transferred to ADP
   - Chemiosmosis: protons move down gradient across membrane

6. **Relative energy of carbohydrates, lipids and proteins**
   - Lipids have higher energy value than carbohydrates or proteins
   - Lipids contain more hydrogen, so more protons / electrons for ETC

7. **Respiratory quotient (RQ)**
   - $$RQ = \frac{\text{volume of } CO_2 \text{ produced}}{\text{volume of } O_2 \text{ taken in}}$$
   - Carbohydrate has highest RQ, lipid lowest
   - Falling RQ may indicate shift from carbohydrate to lipid / protein respiration

8. **Why enter torpor**
   - Winter: reduce heat loss when cold and food limited
   - Summer: conserve water when hot

9. **Respirometer and respiration rate**
   - Can use with and without NaOH
   - NaOH absorbs $$CO_2$$
   - Reduction in gas volume reflects $$O_2$$ uptake
   - Movement of liquid / bubble measured over time
   - Rate = distance or volume change / time

10. **Outline glycolysis**
   - Glucose converted to fructose $$1,6$$-bisphosphate
   - Split into two triose phosphate molecules
   - Converted to two pyruvate molecules
   - 2 ATP used, 4 ATP produced, net gain 2 ATP
   - 2 reduced NAD produced

11. **Why oxygen is required for Krebs cycle**
   - Oxygen is final electron acceptor in ETC
   - Allows reoxidation of NADH to NAD
   - Without NAD, Krebs cycle stops

12. **Link reaction**
   - Pyruvate decarboxylated: $$CO_2$$ released
   - Dehydrogenation: reduced NAD formed
   - Acetate combines with coenzyme A to form acetyl CoA
   - CoA carries acetyl group

13. **Role of NAD and FAD in aerobic respiration**
   - Hydrogen / electron carriers
   - Reduced in link reaction and Krebs cycle
   - Transfer electrons to inner mitochondrial membrane
   - Donate hydrogen / electrons to ETC

14. **ATP synthase in oxidative phosphorylation**
   - Carrier proteins transfer electrons along ETC
   - Energy released pumps protons into intermembrane space
   - Protons move back through ATP synthase
   - ATP formed from ADP and Pi
   - Oxygen is final electron acceptor, forming water

15. **Anaerobic respiration**
   - In animals: pyruvate to lactate by lactate dehydrogenase
   - In yeast / plants: pyruvate to ethanal to ethanol
   - Regenerates NAD

16. **Adaptations of rice to flooded conditions**
   - Aerenchyma
   - Shallow roots
   - Air films on underwater leaves
   - Greater internode growth / leaves above water
   - Anaerobic respiration in submerged tissues
   - Tolerance of ethanol

17. **Investigating anaerobic respiration**
   - Use DCPIP / methylene blue as hydrogen acceptor
   - Blue to colourless as reduced
   - Faster colour change = faster respiration
   - Oil layer added to keep conditions anaerobic

18. **ATP yield reminder**
   - $$1$$ reduced NAD gives about $$2.5$$ ATP
   - $$1$$ reduced FAD gives about $$1.5$$ ATP

## Chapter 13: Photosynthesis

1. **Key pigment facts**
   - Pigments in thylakoid membranes
   - Chlorophyll a and b absorb red light, reflect green
   - Carotene absorbs blue light
   - Carotenoids include carotene and xanthophyll
   - Chlorophyll has magnesium at centre

2. **Photosystem structure**
   - Reaction centre has two chlorophyll a molecules
   - Accessory pigments harvest light and transfer energy to reaction centre

3. **Chromatography reminders**
   - Typical order from top: carotenoids, chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b

4. **Absorption vs action spectrum**
   - Absorption spectrum: degree of absorption
   - Action spectrum: rate of photosynthesis

5. **Experimental variables**
   - Light intensity: change distance of light source
   - Wavelength: use colour filters
   - Carbon dioxide concentration: vary sodium hydrogencarbonate

6. **Dependent variable measurement**
   - Use DCPIP / methylene blue
   - Indicator accepts electrons from ETC
   - Blue to colourless

7. **Products of Calvin cycle intermediates**
   - GP can form amino acids
   - TP can form carbohydrates, lipids and amino acids

8. **How granum structure links to function**
   - Stack of thylakoids gives large surface area
   - Many photosystems / light harvesting clusters
   - Many electron carriers and ATP synthase
   - Site of light-dependent stage / photophosphorylation

9. **Role of photosynthetic pigments**
   - Form photosystems I and II
   - Absorb light energy
   - Excite electrons
   - Accessory pigments pass energy to reaction centre
   - Chlorophyll b acts as accessory pigment

10. **Roles of proteins on thylakoid membrane**
   - Electron carriers transfer electrons
   - Energy released pumps protons into thylakoid space
   - Oxygen-evolving complex splits water
   - ATP synthase synthesises ATP
   - Photosystem proteins hold pigments in place

11. **Cyclic vs non-cyclic photophosphorylation**
   - Cyclic uses PSI only
   - Non-cyclic uses PSI and PSII
   - Cyclic produces ATP only
   - Non-cyclic produces ATP and reduced NADP
   - Photolysis occurs only in non-cyclic

12. **Effect of rubisco denaturing**
   - Active site no longer complementary
   - Cannot fix $$CO_2$$ with RuBP
   - No GP produced
   - No TP produced

## Chapter 14: Homeostasis

1. **Principle of homeostasis**
   - Maintains internal environment near set point
   - Controlled by negative feedback
   - Receptors detect change
   - Coordination system processes information
   - Effectors bring value back toward set point

2. **Negative feedback**
   - Change detected by receptor
   - Message sent to coordination centre
   - Effector produces corrective response
   - Condition returns to set point

3. **Why maintain temperature, glucose, water potential, pH**
   - Prevent hypothermia / overheating
   - Prevent coma from low glucose
   - Prevent dehydration
   - Maintain enzyme function and normal metabolism

4. **Urine formation**
   - Ultrafiltration
   - Selective reabsorption

5. **Ultrafiltration**
   - Afferent arteriole wider than efferent arteriole
   - High hydrostatic pressure in glomerulus
   - Small molecules forced into Bowman’s capsule
   - Endothelium pores and podocyte filtration slits allow passage
   - Basement membrane filters by size

6. **Selective reabsorption in PCT**
   - Sodium-potassium pumps remove sodium from epithelial cells
   - Sodium concentration in cells lowered
   - Sodium enters from filtrate through cotransporters
   - Glucose and amino acids enter with sodium
   - Water follows by osmosis

7. **Adaptations of PCT cells**
   - Microvilli increase surface area
   - Many transport proteins
   - Many mitochondria for ATP
   - Tight junctions reduce leakage

8. **ADH and collecting duct in osmoregulation**
   - Hypothalamus detects change in blood water potential
   - Posterior pituitary releases ADH
   - ADH binds receptors on collecting duct cells
   - Activates signalling pathway involving cAMP and protein kinase
   - Aquaporin-containing vesicles fuse with membrane
   - Membrane becomes more permeable to water
   - Water reabsorbed by osmosis
   - Low water potential: more ADH, small volume concentrated urine
   - High water potential: less ADH, large volume dilute urine

9. **Glucagon and insulin**
   - Insulin stimulates glycogenesis and glycolysis
   - Glucagon stimulates glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis

10. **How adrenaline / glucagon stimulate liver cells to convert glycogen to glucose**
   - Hormone binds receptor
   - Conformational change activates G protein
   - Activates adenyl cyclase
   - cAMP produced
   - Protein kinase activated
   - Enzyme cascade with amplification
   - Glycogen phosphorylase activated
   - Glycogenolysis occurs

11. **Insulin in muscle / adipose cells**
   - Binds receptor
   - Receptor autophosphorylation / signalling cascade
   - GLUT4 vesicles move to membrane
   - Glucose enters by facilitated diffusion
   - Glycogen synthesis stimulated

12. **Insulin in liver cells**
   - Binds receptor
   - Activates glucokinase
   - Glucose phosphorylated
   - More glucose enters by facilitated diffusion
   - Glycogen synthase activated

13. **Urine glucose dipsticks**
   - Use immobilised enzymes: glucose oxidase and peroxidase
   - Hydrogen peroxide formed
   - Chromogen changes colour
   - Compared with colour chart
   - Semi-quantitative only

14. **Glucose biosensor**
   - Blood applied to strip
   - Glucose oxidase catalyses reaction
   - Electron flow measured as current
   - Current proportional to glucose concentration
   - Gives numerical result

15. **Role of ABA in stomatal closure**
   - ABA binds receptor on guard cells
   - Proton pumps inhibited
   - Calcium enters as second messenger
   - Anion channels open, potassium uptake reduced
   - Water leaves by osmosis
   - Guard cells become flaccid, stoma closes

16. **How stomata open**
   - Proton pumps move protons out
   - Potassium enters
   - Water potential decreases
   - Water enters by osmosis
   - Guard cells become turgid and curve apart

17. **Guard cell structure**
   - Thick inner wall, thinner outer wall
   - Folded membrane with many carrier proteins
   - Many mitochondria and chloroplasts
   - Several small vacuoles

## Chapter 15: Control and Coordination

1. **Features of endocrine system**
   - Endocrine glands secrete hormones
   - Hormones transported in blood
   - Act over long distances
   - Responses usually slower than nervous system

2. **Nervous vs endocrine systems**
   - Both transmit information
   - Nervous uses electrical impulses
   - Endocrine uses chemical messengers
   - Nervous is faster, endocrine longer-lasting

3. **Structure of motor neurone**
   - Dendrites attached to cell body
   - Nucleus in cell body
   - Long axon
   - Many mitochondria
   - Terminal branches
   - Myelin sheath by Schwann cells
   - Nodes of Ranvier
   - Cell body in CNS

4. **Sensory neurone**
   - Cell body in ganglion outside spinal cord
   - Interneurones only in CNS

5. **Sensory receptor cells**
   - Convert stimulus into electrical impulse
   - Can be specialised receptor cells or sensory neurone endings

6. **Resting potential**
   - Sodium-potassium pump moves $$3\,Na^+$$ out and $$2\,K^+$$ in
   - Membrane more permeable to potassium
   - Potassium diffuses out
   - Inside becomes more negative

7. **Transmission of action potential**
   - Stimulus opens voltage-gated sodium channels
   - Sodium enters, membrane depolarises
   - Threshold reached, more sodium channels open
   - Potassium channels then open
   - Potassium leaves, membrane repolarises

8. **Myelin sheath**
   - Many layers of Schwann cell membrane
   - Electrical insulation
   - Increases speed of transmission
   - Saltatory conduction between nodes of Ranvier

9. **Unmyelinated neurones**
   - Local currents depolarise adjacent membrane sections
   - Continuous conduction

10. **Refractory period**
   - Sodium channels inactivated / closed
   - Sodium-potassium pump restores resting potential
   - Ensures one-way transmission
   - Sets maximum impulse frequency

11. **Roles of synapses**
   - One-way transmission
   - Communication between neurones
   - Integration of signals

12. **Cholinergic synapse process**
   - Action potential arrives
   - Calcium channels open
   - Calcium enters presynaptic terminal
   - Vesicles fuse with membrane
   - Acetylcholine released
   - Acetylcholine binds receptors on postsynaptic membrane
   - Sodium channels open, postsynaptic membrane depolarises
   - Acetylcholinesterase breaks down acetylcholine
   - Choline reabsorbed and reused

13. **Muscle structure reminders**
   - Thin filament: actin, troponin, tropomyosin
   - Thick filament: myosin
   - Sarcolemma: muscle cell membrane
   - Sarcoplasm: cytoplasm
   - Sarcoplasmic reticulum stores calcium ions

14. **Roles of neuromuscular junction, T-tubule system and SR**
   - NMJ transmits impulse to muscle fibre
   - T-tubules carry impulse deep into fibre
   - SR stores and releases calcium ions

15. **Sliding filament model**
   - Action potential causes acetylcholine release
   - Sarcolemma depolarises
   - Action potential travels down T-tubules
   - Calcium released from SR
   - Calcium binds troponin
   - Tropomyosin moves away from binding sites
   - Myosin heads bind actin and pull
   - ATP hydrolysis breaks cross-bridge
   - Cycle repeats

16. **Venus fly trap rapid response**
   - Sensory hairs stimulated
   - Generator potential / action potential produced
   - Cell wall loosening and ion changes occur
   - Water enters cells by osmosis
   - Trap changes shape and closes

17. **Role of auxin in elongation growth**
   - Auxin activates proton pumps
   - Cell wall acidified and loosened
   - Potassium enters
   - Water enters by osmosis
   - Cell elongates

18. **Role of gibberellin in barley germination**
   - Water uptake stimulates embryo
   - Gibberellin produced
   - Diffuses to aleurone layer
   - Amylase synthesised
   - Starch hydrolysed to sugars for respiration

19. **Control of gibberellin synthesis / stem elongation**
   - Dominant allele codes for functional enzyme
   - Active gibberellin produced
   - Gibberellin binds receptor
   - DELLA protein destroyed
   - Transcription factor activates genes
   - Cell walls loosen and elongation occurs

## Chapter 16: Inheritance

1. **Definitions**
   - Haploid: one set of chromosomes
   - Diploid: two sets of chromosomes
   - Homologous chromosomes: same genes at same loci

2. **Why fertilisation produces diploid cell with homologous chromosomes**
   - Fusion of two haploid gametes
   - One chromosome set from each parent

3. **Genetic diagram reminders**
   - Include parental genotypes
   - Gametes
   - Offspring genotypes
   - Offspring phenotypes
   - Ratio if required

4. **Patterns of inheritance**
   - Autosomal dominant / recessive
   - Sex-linked dominant / recessive
   - Codominance
   - Incomplete dominance
   - Epistasis
   - Polygenic inheritance

5. **Need for reduction division in meiosis**
   - Halves chromosome number
   - Prevents chromosome number doubling each generation

6. **Meiosis I**
   - Prophase I: homologous chromosomes pair, crossing over
   - Metaphase I: bivalents line up at equator
   - Anaphase I: homologous chromosomes separate
   - Telophase I: chromosomes reach poles

7. **Meiosis II**
   - Similar to mitosis
   - Sister chromatids separate
   - Four haploid daughter cells formed

8. **Triploid plants are sterile because**
   - Odd number of chromosomes
   - Cannot pair in prophase I
   - No normal meiosis / gamete formation

9. **How meiosis causes genetic variation**
   - Crossing over in prophase I
   - Independent assortment in metaphase I and II
   - Random fertilisation

10. **Gene-related terms**
   - Gene: length of DNA coding for polypeptide / protein
   - Locus: position of gene on chromosome
   - Allele: alternative form of gene

11. **Other definitions**
   - Dominant, recessive, codominant
   - Phenotype, genotype
   - Homozygous, heterozygous
   - Test cross
   - Linkage
   - Epistasis

12. **Chi-squared test**
   - Compares observed and expected values
   - For discontinuous data

13. **t-test**
   - Compares means of two samples
   - Continuous data

14. **Genetic diseases examples**
   - HBB: sickle cell anaemia
   - TYR: albinism
   - F8: haemophilia
   - HTT: Huntington’s disease

15. **Control of gibberellin synthesis**
   - Dominant allele codes for functional enzyme
   - Active gibberellin produced

16. **Regulatory gene / structural gene**
   - Structural gene codes for functional protein
   - Regulatory gene controls expression of other genes

17. **Lac operon**
   - No lactose: repressor binds operator, transcription prevented
   - Lactose present: lactose binds repressor, transcription occurs

18. **Role of transcription factors**
   - Bind promoter
   - Allow or prevent transcription
   - Involved in sex determination, cell cycle, response to environment

19. **How GA stimulates stem elongation**
   - DELLA inhibits transcription factor
   - GA binds receptor
   - DELLA destroyed
   - Transcription factor activates genes
   - Cell walls loosen, elongation occurs

## Chapter 17: Selection and Evolution

1. **Natural selection occurs because**
   - More offspring produced than survive
   - Competition for resources
   - Better adapted individuals survive and reproduce
   - Advantageous alleles passed on

2. **Selection pressure**
   - Environmental factor affecting survival and reproduction

3. **Types of selection**
   - Stabilising
   - Directional
   - Disruptive

4. **How selection / drift / founder / bottleneck affect allele frequency**
   - Natural selection changes frequency of advantageous alleles
   - Genetic drift changes allele frequency by chance, especially in small populations
   - Founder effect: small group forms new population
   - Bottleneck: population size greatly reduced

5. **Explaining maintained allele frequency**
   - Identify selection pressure
   - State advantage of allele
   - Survivors reproduce and pass on allele

6. **When migration is involved**
   - Founder effect important
   - Migrants may carry allele at higher frequency
   - Small gene pool increases drift effect

7. **Antibiotic resistance in bacteria**
   - Random mutation gives resistance
   - Non-resistant bacteria die
   - Resistant bacteria survive and reproduce asexually
   - Resistance allele passed on

8. **Hardy-Weinberg conditions**
   - No selection
   - No migration
   - Random mating
   - Large population
   - No mutation

9. **Selective breeding / artificial selection**
   - Select parents with desired phenotype
   - Breed them
   - Repeat over generations
   - Desired alleles increase in frequency

10. **Theory of evolution**
   - New species arise from pre-existing species over time
   - Gene pools change across generations

11. **Why mtDNA is useful for relationships**
   - Inherited maternally
   - No crossing over
   - Mutates at fairly constant rate
   - Often faster mutation rate than nuclear DNA

12. **Speciation by geographical isolation**
   - Barrier separates populations
   - No gene flow
   - Different selection pressures
   - Accumulated differences prevent interbreeding

13. **Sympatric speciation**
   - No geographic barrier
   - Ecological or behavioural isolation
   - Gene pools diverge until interbreeding impossible

14. **Species definition**
   - Organisms with similar characteristics
   - Can interbreed to produce fertile offspring

## Chapter 18: Biodiversity and Conservation

1. **Role of IUCN and CITES**
   - IUCN ranks threat level and raises awareness
   - CITES controls trade in endangered species and products

2. **Ways of conserving animals**
   - Captive breeding
   - Raising awareness
   - Penalties for hunting
   - National parks

3. **Reasons for maintaining diversity / biodiversity**
   - Maintain genetic diversity
   - Ethical reasons
   - Aesthetic reasons
   - Economic / research value
   - Stability of food chains and ecosystems

4. **Taxonomic hierarchy**
   - Kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species
   - Genus capitalised, species lower case, both italicised

5. **Features of kingdom fungi**
   - Eukaryotic
   - No chloroplasts
   - Heterotrophic
   - Cell wall of chitin
   - Usually multicellular, yeast unicellular

6. **What to monitor in conservation of new species**
   - Habitat size
   - Hunting pressure
   - Food source
   - Genetic diversity

7. **Differences between archaea and bacteria**
   - Different membrane lipids
   - Different rRNA types
   - Cell wall composition differs

8. **Meaning of biodiversity**
   - Variety of organisms in community
   - Variation within species
   - Genetic diversity between species
   - Range of habitats / niches

9. **Why tropical rainforests have high biodiversity**
   - High rate of photosynthesis
   - Many plant species
   - Food available year-round
   - Many niches

10. **Using frame quadrat to assess plant biodiversity**
   - Use quadrat of known size
   - Lay grid over study area
   - Select random coordinates
   - Identify species with key
   - Count abundance
   - Repeat sampling
   - Calculate Simpson’s Index

11. **Correlation reminders**
   - Null hypothesis: no correlation between variables
   - If test statistic greater than critical value, reject null hypothesis

## Chapter 19: Gene Technology

1. **Role of Taq polymerase in PCR**
   - DNA polymerase
   - Extends primers to form complementary strands
   - Works best around $$72^\circ C$$
   - Not denatured at high temperature used in PCR
   - Reusable across many cycles

2. **Why specific primers are used**
   - Bind targeted DNA region

3. **How gel electrophoresis works**
   - DNA fragments placed in wells
   - DNA negatively charged
   - Move toward anode
   - Gel acts as molecular sieve
   - Smaller fragments move further

4. **Longer gel electrophoresis answer**
   - Desired DNA amplified by PCR
   - DNA may be cut by restriction enzymes
   - Fragments loaded into gel
   - Current applied
   - Fragments separate by size

5. **How gene can be transferred between bacteria**
   - Via plasmids

6. **How DNA sequencing results are analysed**
   - Use databases / bioinformatics
   - Compare fossil DNA sequences with known sequences
   - Identify similarities / genes

7. **How microarrays detect disease by gene expression**
   - Extract mRNA
   - Convert to cDNA
   - Label cDNA with fluorescent marker
   - Hybridise to probes on microarray
   - Fluorescence indicates expression level

8. **Ways to obtain a gene**
   - Synthesize from known nucleotide sequence
   - Make cDNA from mRNA using reverse transcriptase
   - Extract from DNA with restriction enzymes

9. **Recombinant DNA**
   - DNA joined from two different sources

10. **CRISPR / gene editing**
   - Find nucleotide sequence
   - Use guide RNA for specific target
   - Insert / delete / substitute nucleotides
   - Change triplets / codons
   - Change amino acid sequence / protein produced
   - Must be specific to mutant gene to avoid damaging others

11. **Why a graph may level off**
   - X-axis factor no longer limiting
   - Another factor becomes limiting
